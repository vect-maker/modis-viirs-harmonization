## 1. Challenge Overview

Understanding how burning activity is distributed across our planet over time—including wildfires and agricultural burns intense enough to be detected by satellite sensors—enables us to anticipate critical periods, guide monitoring efforts, and improve fire response.

Satellites have tracked active fire hotspots for more than two decades, but the record is split across sensors whose data cannot be compared directly, leaving fire managers without a consistent picture of when and where burning has occurred.

**The Challenge:** Build a web application that harmonizes these records into a **burning activity calendar** based on active fire hotspots, enabling early warning and emergency responders, scientists, and land managers to examine historical fire patterns, unusual conditions, and critical periods within a selected area of interest.

## 2. Background

### Fire Regimes
* Some ecosystems need periodic fires to maintain their function and their structure. However, changes in fire intensity, extent, and frequency can have negative impacts on climate, society, and biodiversity, along with the ecosystem services they provide.
* Fires can cause loss of life and property, degradation of health, and acute risk to fire managers.
* Because humans have modified global patterns of vegetation, ignition, and climate over millennia, fires on both local and global scales are increasingly human-related.
* Burning activity involves wildfires in natural vegetation and forested areas, as well as anthropogenic fires associated with agriculture, land-clearing, waste disposal, and industrial activities. It is one of the many dynamic phenomena detectable with remote sensing.

### Satellite Fire Detection and Monitoring
* Climate models project that fire weather will become more common and more intense.
* Earth Observation (EO) satellites provide technological solutions for early detection, rapid response, and post-fire assessment, measuring thermal radiation and smoke across regional to global scales.

### Detecting Hotspots on Earth's Surface
* EO satellites provide information on fire location, intensity, and temporal evolution.
* Satellite sensors operating in the thermal infrared (TIR) range can detect hotspots on Earth's surface generated by urban fires, wildfires, volcanic eruptions, and industrial processes (such as gas flares in oil and gas operations).
* For active fires, detection and monitoring rely mainly on mid-infrared (MIR) and TIR measurements, where active combustion produces its strongest signal.
* Sensors such as **MODIS** and **VIIRS** provide real-time thermal anomaly detection, enabling early hotspot identification and the assessment of burned areas.

### Burn Activity Calendars & The Core Problem
* Using satellite-detected hotspots, fire activity calendars can be developed by integrating historical information with recent data, allowing for rapid visual identification of seasonal patterns, anomalies, and trends in each region.
* **The Root of the Challenge:** MODIS sensors on board the Terra and Aqua satellites provide more than two decades of observations, but they are approaching the end of their operational life and are being replaced by VIIRS sensors.
* Their records **cannot be directly compared without harmonization**, because MODIS and VIIRS have different:
  1. **Spatial resolutions** (e.g., MODIS ~1 km vs. VIIRS 375 m / 750 m)
  2. **Sensor sensitivities**
  3. **Observation schedules & overpass times**
* As fire frequency and intensity increase, emergency managers benefit from an updated and consistent hotspot calendar that enables them to rapidly visualize:
  - Which months and years had the highest fire activity.
  - How current month burning activity compares against the historical median for a selected area.
* Harmonizing NASA Earthdata FIRMS hotspots combines the extensive historical series of MODIS with the continuity and greater detail of VIIRS.
* Emergency managers can then use the web application to monitor sensitive regions, recognize changes relative to historical patterns, and strengthen fire forecasting and response.

## 3. Objectives

The primary objective is to develop a harmonized burning activity calendar based on active fire hotspots in a web application, combining the extensive historical series of MODIS with the continuity and greater detail of VIIRS to provide a consistent time series of hotspot activity.

This challenge is approached in **two main parts**:

### Part 1: MODIS & VIIRS Hotspot Harmonization
* Harmonize active fire data across MODIS and VIIRS so that the sensor of origin no longer matters, allowing analytical focus to center on actual patterns, trends, and anomalies rather than sensor artifacts.
* Bridge the transition period (2012–present) where both MODIS and VIIRS overlap, reconciling differences in spatial resolution, sensitivity, and detection frequency.

### Part 2: Web Application & Burning Activity Calendar
* Build an interactive visual web application that consumes the harmonized hotspot data as input.
* Allow users to select a region of interest and a specific date range.
* Provide an integrated **burning activity calendar** showing the number of hotspots detected from the early 2000s to the present.
* Enable emergency managers to answer:
  - Which periods of the year see more fires?
  - Which years had more burning activity?
  - How does a current fire / fire season compare with historical baselines (e.g., historical median)?
* Provide accessible, intuitive visualizations:
  - Color ramps representing similarities and differences in hotspot counts across months.
  - Downloadable analytics and visualizations of fire activity.
  - Downloadable CSV file of hotspots detected during a user query/search.

## 4. Potential Considerations & Best Practices

* **Target Audience:** Early warning and emergency responders, scientists, land managers, and firefighters.
* **Interactivity:** Interactive map interface where users can draw custom areas of interest (bounding box / polygon) or enter coordinates (latitude / longitude).
* **Accessible Visualization:** Use accessible, color-blind-friendly color ramps for heatmaps and calendar charts.
* **Key Indices & Figures to Highlight:**
  - Historical comparisons and anomalies against multi-year averages/medians.
  - Total number of fires detected and reported over time.
* **Data Export:** Export capabilities for filtered subsets (e.g., downloadable CSV of detected hotspots for a search region).
