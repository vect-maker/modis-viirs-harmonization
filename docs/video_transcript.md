## Full Script / Transcript

**[00:03 - 00:19] Introduction & Context**
> "Hello, I'm Marina Compagnucci from CONAE, the Argentinian Space Agency. Right now, we are at the Teófilo Tabanera Space Center located in Córdoba, Argentina. I'm an author of the challenge called Harmonization of MODIS and VIIRS Hotspots."

**[00:20 - 00:46] The Role of Early Warning & Hotspots**
> "You know, I have the privilege of working with a team of scientists that provide early warning and emergency response to various events happening around the globe. Those events are fires, floodings, oil spills that can last from a few days to even weeks. Almost half of those emergencies are fires—either wildfires from forested areas or human-related fires from agricultural activities, industrial activities, or land clearing."

**[00:47 - 01:21] Burn Activity Calendars**
> "In the case of ongoing fires, these scientists use satellites' hotspots to monitor them and to know where the highest probabilities of active fires are. But there is another powerful tool that also uses hotspots—in this case, integrating historical and recent data coming from hotspots. This tool is called a **burning activity calendar** (also a fire activity calendar). And it helps visualize trends, anomalies, or even seasonal patterns in fire behavior. For example, it shows you how many hotspots a particular month has across different years. Consider that climate models are projecting that fire weather will become more common and more intense, so these tools—both the calendar and hotspots—are really important."

**[01:22 - 01:47] Real-World Case Study (Chilecito, La Rioja, Argentina)**
> "The images you're watching were processed for a fire that happened in Chilecito, La Rioja, Argentina in July 2026. At the beginning of the event, scientists used hotspots to inspect the area and wait for a satellite to pass. Then, with the images, they can work with more detail. But hotspots remain important since they could identify areas where fires may or may not spread. By the end of the event, they have more detail of the burned area."

**[01:48 - 02:30] The Core Discrepancy & Retiring Sensors**
> "Now, we have finally arrived at the core of the challenge: even though MODIS and VIIRS sensors have tracked active fire hotspots for more than two decades, the problem is this record is split among these two different sensors whose data cannot be directly compared.
> 
> Why? Because they have:
> 1. Different spatial resolution
> 2. Different sensitivities
> 3. Different observation schedules
> 
> And to make matters worse, MODIS sensors are retiring from their operational life and being replaced by VIIRS sensors. So, if there is no method to compare their data, historical continuity will be compromised, and fire managers will be left without a historical calendar that tells them when and where fires occur. That is why we need your help with this real and pressing problem."

**[02:31 - 03:20] Challenge Deliverables & Two Phases**
> "Your challenge, should you choose it, is to develop a harmonized burn activity calendar that combines the extensive historical information from MODIS with the continuity and greater detail of VIIRS, so that by the end of this challenge, we have a consistent time series of hotspot activity—all this in a web application.
> 
> Consider using NASA FIRMS website and resources coming from our agency and other agencies to approach this challenge.
> 
> When we were thinking about the challenge, we thought of it as having two parts to approach:
> 1. **Hotspot Harmonization:** Harmonization of hotspots from MODIS and VIIRS, so that by the end of this phase, it doesn't matter if a hotspot came from one sensor or the other—the real focus is on the trends, anomalies, and patterns they unveil.
> 2. **Web Application & Calendar:** The development of a web application that takes these hotspots as input and pours them into a calendar so that a user, for example, can choose a specific date, a particular region, and visualize its fire activity history, with analytics.
> 
> Or maybe you and your team decide to approach this challenge in a completely different manner, and it's really successful. We're really looking forward to what your team comes up with. Cheers and good luck!"
